Taco Cam: set it up

Taco Cam turns your Android phone into the camera for your Whatnot stream in OBS. The phone plugs into the PC with its USB cable. Do the setup once. After that it's plug in and go.

What you need

  • A Windows 10 or 11 PC with OBS Studio on it (free from obsproject.com). Open OBS once and close it before you start.
  • An Android phone with Android 11 or newer, and a USB cable that carries data. Some cheap cables only charge. If the PC doesn't notice the phone at all, try another cable.
  • The file TacoCam.zip or TacoCam-Setup-1.0.exe (the installer). Both give you the same Taco Cam. Got the installer? Do "1. Or: the installer" below instead of step 1.

1. Unzip and start Taco Cam

a. Right-click TacoCam.zip and click Properties. If there's an Unblock box at the bottom, tick it and click OK. That stops Windows asking about the files later.

b. Right-click TacoCam.zip again, click Extract All..., then Extract. Put it somewhere it can stay, like Documents. A folder called Taco Cam opens.

c. Double-click Start Taco Cam. The Taco Cam window opens, with a smaller window called Get started on top of it.

If Windows asks whether to run it, click Run. If it says "Windows protected your PC", click More info, then Run anyway. Windows asks because the start file is new to it. The programs inside are signed by the Python Software Foundation and by Google.

1. Or: the installer

a. Double-click TacoCam-Setup-1.0.exe. Windows will probably say "Windows protected your PC": click More info, then Run anyway. It says that because the installer isn't signed with a paid certificate. If you'd rather not see that, use the zip.

b. Click through the installer (no admin password needed). At the end, leave Open Taco Cam now ticked and click Finish. The Taco Cam window opens with Get started on top.

Next time, open Taco Cam from the Start menu or the desktop icon. To remove it: Windows Settings, Apps, Taco Cam, Uninstall.

2. The three steps in "Get started"

Each step turns green when it's done. You can open this window again any time with the Get started button under the picture.

Step 1: Install the Taco Cam camera

Click Install the camera. Windows asks "Do you want to allow this app to make changes to your device?" Click Yes. When the black window says "Done", press a key to close it, then close Taco Cam and start it again.

This is what lets OBS see Taco Cam as a camera. You only do it once. The black window also says to add a "Video Capture Device" in OBS: skip that, step 3 adds the camera for you.

Step 2: Connect the phone

The phone needs one setting switched on, called USB debugging. It lets the PC put the Taco Cam app on the phone and talk to it over the cable.

  1. On the phone open Settings > About phone. Tap Build number 7 times. It says "You are now a developer".
    Samsung: Settings > About phone > Software information > Build number.
  2. Go back to Settings and open Developer options. On most phones it's under Settings > System. On Samsung it's at the bottom of Settings. Switch on USB debugging.
  3. Plug the phone into the PC. The phone asks "Allow USB debugging?". Tick Always allow from this computer and tap Allow.

That's it. Taco Cam installs its app on the phone and opens it, which takes about 10 seconds. The phone shows the camera and "Connected to the PC", and the Taco Cam window shows the picture.

Xiaomi, Redmi and POCO phones also need Install via USB switched on in Developer options.

Step 3: Set up OBS

Close OBS first. Then click Set up OBS. It makes a profile and scene collection called Taco Cam Whatnot:

  • the tall 1080 x 1920 picture Whatnot uses
  • Whatnot's stream settings
  • the Taco Cam camera already in the "Live" scene, turned the right way
  • your microphone

Under the button you pick how OBS turns the picture into the stream. On a PC with an NVIDIA graphics card, Taco Cam picks NVIDIA (NVENC): the graphics card does that work, so the PC's processor stays free. Standard (x264) works on any PC. Both send the stream the way Whatnot asks for it.

With NVIDIA: Whatnot's Update Profile makes its own profile called "Whatnot Live (Recommended)" with Standard (x264) and switches OBS to it. To use NVIDIA again, close OBS, open Get started and click Re-apply NVIDIA settings.

If OBS's connection for other programs was off, Taco Cam switches it on and shows you its password. It also copies the password, so you can paste it. Whatnot's Show Tools asks for this password. You can find it again in OBS under Tools > WebSocket Server Settings.

Open OBS. You should see the "Live" scene with your phone's picture filling it.

3. Every show

  1. Start Taco Cam (double-click Start Taco Cam).
  2. Plug in the phone and unlock it. The Taco Cam app opens by itself. Leave it on the phone's screen. Android stops the camera of apps that aren't on screen. The Dim button on the phone darkens its screen to save battery.
  3. Open OBS.
  4. On whatnot.com, in Chrome, open Show Tools for your show. Connect OBS with the password from step 3, then click Update Profile. Whatnot fills in your stream key.
  5. Click Pre-show check in Taco Cam. It lists anything that still needs fixing, like a phone that isn't charging, a phone that's too hot, or a missing camera. It also runs by itself when OBS starts streaming.

Turn on Do Not Disturb on the phone. A call would take the camera away in the middle of the show.

If something's wrong

Taco Cam says "Plug the Android phone into this PC" but the phone is plugged in
Try another USB cable or another USB port. On the phone, pull down from the top and tap the USB message; pick File transfer. Check USB debugging is still on. Samsung phones may need Samsung's USB driver on the PC (search "Samsung Android USB driver" on samsung.com).
The phone never asked "Allow USB debugging?"
Unplug the phone and plug it in again with the phone unlocked. Still nothing: in Developer options tap Revoke USB debugging authorizations, then plug it in again.
Taco Cam says "the phone blocked installing over USB"
On Xiaomi, Redmi or POCO switch on Install via USB in Developer options (it may ask you to sign in to a Mi account), then unplug and plug the phone in again.
The picture is sideways in OBS
Taco Cam sends a wide picture on purpose, and the OBS scene turns it. In Taco Cam's Output (OBS) tab, Rotate must stay on 0. If you added the camera to OBS yourself, click Set up OBS again.
The Whatnot web page shows a sideways, zoomed camera with no overlays
The Whatnot tab in your browser is using the Taco Cam camera itself instead of your OBS stream. Click the camera icon in the address bar, set Camera to Block for whatnot.com, and reload the page.
The picture freezes or the phone says the camera stopped
Keep the Taco Cam app on the phone's screen, and close video-call apps. If the phone is hot, take it out of its case and use the Dim button.
My browser or antivirus says "Virus detected"
That's a false alarm about the zip. Every program in it is signed by the Python Software Foundation or Google. Taco Cam's own part is plain text files you can read. Ask the person who sent it for a fresh copy, or let your antivirus keep the file.

Removing Taco Cam

  • The camera: in the Taco Cam folder open pc > vcam and double-click Uninstall Taco Cam camera.
  • The PC program: delete the Taco Cam folder.
  • The phone: uninstall the Taco Cam app like any app, and switch off USB debugging if you like.
  • OBS: the Taco Cam Whatnot profile and scene collection can be removed from OBS's Profile and Scene Collection menus.
